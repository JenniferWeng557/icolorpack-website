## 1. The Physics of Warping: Why Do Lids Bend?
Warping (or bowing) of rigid box panels is the most common and difficult defect to control. It is caused entirely by **moisture and tension imbalance** between the inner liner paper and the outer wrap paper.

*   **The Grain Direction Trap:** Paper is highly anisotropic; it expands and contracts significantly more across its grain direction than along it. If a factory laminates the inner liner and outer wrap paper with their grain directions perpendicular to each other, they will pull in opposite directions as the glue dries, warping the rigid board.
*   **Substrate Tension Mismatch:** If the outer wrap is a heavy 150gsm textured paper and the inner liner is a light 80gsm offset paper, the heavier outer paper will pull with greater force, bending the box panel outward.

---

## 2. The Root of Corner Splitting & Delamination
Two other defects frequently ruin the unboxing experience of luxury products:

*   **Corner Splitting:** The sharp 90-degree corners of a rigid box put intense tension on the wrap paper. In dry climates (such as heated warehouses in North America or Northern Europe during winter), the paper loses moisture, shrinks, and splits open along the edges, exposing the grayboard core. (To see how we apply this structural engineering to folding designs, see our guide on [Collapsible Magnetic Gift Boxes](shipping-cost-pain-point-packaging.html)).
*   **Delamination & Bubbling:** This occurs when air becomes trapped between the wrap paper and the grayboard. It is caused by using cheap, fast-cooling hot-melt glue that solidifies before the box is fully pressed, or by inadequate pressure during the wrapping phase.
