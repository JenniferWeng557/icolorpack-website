1. When does low MOQ custom packaging make sense?

Low MOQ custom packaging can help a growing brand test a product, seasonal design or new market without committing to unnecessary inventory. The practical minimum depends on the box or bag structure, dimensions, board or paper availability, printing method, finishes and artwork versions. At iColorPacks, all product types can be ordered from 100 pieces per size and artwork. Small-batch unit prices are higher because setup costs are spread across fewer pieces. We recommend comparing a 500-piece order for better value. Request 100, 500 and 1,000 pieces against the same size, artwork and finish so you can compare unit price, total spend and storage needs.

4. Digital printing vs. offset printing

Digital printing can suit short runs, variable artwork and projects where avoiding plates matters. Offset printing can suit repeat production, demanding color control and quantities that justify setup. The correct choice depends on substrate, print area, finish, approved color standard and order quantity; lead time and unit-price differences should be confirmed in the written quotation.

5. FAQ: What B2B Buyers Ask Most

Q: Can a small-batch order use foil, UV or embossing?
A: These finishes may be possible, but each one can add tooling, setup or material requirements. Confirm feasibility and MOQ against the selected structure, artwork and quantity.
Q: Can the sampling fee be credited towards my bulk order?
A: The sample-making fee is quoted for your design and specifications. It can be refunded after you place the bulk order; the amount and refund arrangement are stated in the quotation before payment. Ask for sample delivery charges to be listed separately.
Q: Is air, courier or sea freight better for a small order?
A: Compare packed volume and weight, destination, required arrival date, customs terms and handling risk. Bulk production usually takes around 15 days, with the start date and schedule confirmed after specifications, artwork and sample approval are agreed. Sampling and shipping time are separate. The freight route is selected from the packed shipment data and your required arrival date.
Q: What information is needed to confirm MOQ?

A: Send the internal size, structure, material, print coverage, finishes, insert requirements, artwork versions and destination. MOQ is confirmed after these specifications are reviewed.

Low MOQ custom packaging: one design or several?

A small launch can contain several print versions. Before comparing quotations, list both the total quantity and the quantity for each artwork. A shared box size does not automatically mean shared printing or finishing setup.

Compare structures for your quote: for a wholesale rigid gift box brief, review magnetic closure boxes and sliding drawer boxes. For custom paper bags with logo, review ribbon handles, matte lamination and metallic branding. Send the chosen reference with your dimensions and quantity per artwork.

Compare two routes for a multi-design launch

  • Separate printed versions: specify the quantity of each design. Ask which tooling can be shared and which printing, foil or finishing charges repeat.
  • One common pack with different labels or sleeves: ask whether a shared base structure is practical. Include the labels, application work and final appearance in the comparison. A suitable stock size may be unavailable, so confirm fit first.

For example, a launch with three artworks needs three quantity lines in the quotation, even if all three use the same dimensions. This is a planning example; feasibility and minimum quantities require a specification review.

Compare the delivered project cost

Request the same breakdown from every supplier: sampling, tooling, printing setup, production, inserts or labels, assembly, export packing and freight. State the destination and agreed delivery term so you can identify any charges excluded from the quotation. Divide the comparable total by the number of usable finished packs you need.

For rigid boxes, ask for packed carton dimensions, carton count and gross weight for both an assembled and a collapsible option where structurally suitable. Include assembly work at your destination; a lower freight volume alone does not settle the total cost.

Send a version-by-version brief

Include: product and internal size; structure; quantity per artwork; shared and different components; print and finish; destination; target arrival date. Ask the supplier to identify assumptions before you approve a sample.
