How to Choose a Custom Packaging Manufacturer: 7 Checks Before You Order

Published July 11, 2026 · Reading time: 6 min

Choosing a custom packaging manufacturer means comparing more than a unit price. Confirm who will make the packaging, what is included in the specification, how you will approve a sample, and who is responsible for each stage of delivery.

This checklist is for buyers sourcing custom paper bags, rigid gift boxes, bakery packaging and corrugated mailers. Use it before paying for a sample or approving bulk production.

Seven checks before you order

01

Verify the supplier and production route

Ask for the legal company name, production address and a clear description of which steps are done in-house and which are subcontracted. Recent, relevant production photos or a video call can help you understand the process. A supplier may use partners; what matters is that the arrangement is explained and one party is accountable for your order.

02

Put the complete specification in writing

Record the finished dimensions and whether they are internal or external, paper or board, construction, printing, finishes, inserts, handle style, quantity per design and packing method. Ask the supplier to list sample charges, tooling, unit price and freight separately so you can compare like for like.

03

Compare MOQ and price breaks on the same design

Minimum quantities and unit prices depend on the product, size, material and setup. Compare offers using the same artwork and specification at each quantity. iColorPacks' stated minimum is 100 pieces per size and artwork; small batches have a higher unit price because setup costs are spread over fewer pieces.

04

Choose the right sample and test the actual product

Clarify whether you are reviewing an existing reference sample or ordering a custom sample. For a custom sample, confirm the fee, shipping cost and any refund arrangement in the written quotation. Before approval, check fit, opening and closure, print color, foil or embossing, handle attachment and insert layout using the product you plan to pack.

05

Agree what approval and inspection will cover

Keep the approved artwork version and sample specification with the order. Agree how changes will be recorded, what checks will be made before packing, and what photos or inspection records you will receive. For a color-critical or fit-sensitive project, state the acceptance criteria before production starts.

06

Confirm sampling, production and transit separately

Ask for the schedule for each stage after the structure, materials, finish, quantity, artwork and approval route are known. Include your required arrival date and destination. A production estimate alone does not include sample approval time or international transit.

07

Write down shipping responsibilities and total cost

Give the supplier the delivery country and postcode, then confirm the shipping term, freight method, export packing and which charges are included. Ask who arranges transport, customs documentation and import duties. Compare the landed cost and responsibilities, not just the factory unit price.

What to send with your first inquiry

A useful quote request can be brief. Include:

  • Product dimensions and packed weight; say whether packaging measurements are internal or external.
  • Quantity per size and artwork, plus your preferred material and construction.
  • Logo or reference artwork, print colors, finishes and insert requirements.
  • Delivery country and postcode, shipping preference if known, and required arrival date.
  • Whether you need an existing sample, a blank structural sample or a custom printed sample.

How to compare supplier replies

Look for a response that identifies missing details, separates cost items and explains what must be approved before production. Be cautious if the quote omits the material or construction, changes the specification without saying so, or leaves delivery charges and responsibilities unclear. Ask for a revised written quotation before paying.
