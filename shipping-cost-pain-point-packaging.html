Stop Shipping Empty Space: Packaging Freight and Landed Cost

Measure the complete pack-out—not only the unit box price—before deciding between assembled rigid boxes, collapsible structures, folding cartons and corrugated mailers.

Updated September 28, 2026 · iColorPacks Editorial Team

Why package volume can outweigh unit price

International freight is not decided by the packaging purchase price. Depending on the route and service, a shipment may be charged using actual weight, shipment volume or a dimensional-weight rule. A light but bulky pack can therefore create a higher logistics bill than its material weight suggests.

Pre-assembled rigid boxes contain open interior space during shipment. Collapsible rigid boxes, folding cartons and corrugated mailers can travel flat, but they may add assembly labor, adhesive systems or structural limitations. The correct decision depends on the complete packing and fulfillment process.

Avoid percentage promises without pack-out data. A statement such as “save 70% on freight” is not responsible until the assembled and flat alternatives have confirmed export-carton dimensions, carton count, gross weight, route and trade term.

Measurements to request before comparing freight

Ask every supplier to quote the same packaging specification and provide the shipment data below. Product dimensions alone are not enough.

Finished package length × width × height
Flat or assembled packing dimensions
Units per export carton
Export-carton length × width × height
Net and gross weight per carton
Total carton count for the quoted quantity
Pallet dimensions and pallet count, if required
Incoterm, destination and included charges

A simple shipment-volume check is:

Total shipment CBM = export-carton length × width × height in metres × total carton count.
Confirm the carrier or freight forwarder’s chargeable-weight method separately because route and service rules vary.

Do not compare a premium rigid presentation box and a shipping mailer as if they perform the same job. Start with the product load, sales channel, expected handling and customer experience, then compare structures that meet the same requirement.

Calculate landed cost per accepted usable pack

The cheapest factory unit price is not necessarily the lowest-cost result. Use the same destination, quantity, specification and forecast period for every option.

Landed cost per accepted pack = (production + tooling + samples + export packing + freight + duty/tax + local delivery + expected damage or rework) ÷ accepted usable units.

Build a freight-ready packaging RFQ

Send the packaging factory enough information to develop the pack and enough destination information to prepare useful logistics data. A freight-ready request should include:

Product dimensions, weight and fragility
Required quantity for each size and artwork
Preferred structure and reference images
Material, print and finishing requirements
Whether flat packing is acceptable
Fulfillment or assembly limitations
Delivery country, postcode or port
Target arrival date and preferred trade term

For projects combining boxes, inserts and bags, the OEM custom packaging workflow helps organize one coordinated specification.

Common buyer questions

Does flat packing always reduce freight cost?

No. It can reduce shipment volume, but the result must be calculated using the actual export-carton dimensions, units per carton, total carton count, gross weight, route and freight terms.

Can I compare two suppliers using only unit price and CBM?

No. Confirm that size, material, print, finish, insert, packing method, usable yield and trade term match. Otherwise the quotations may describe different products.

What should a physical sample approve?

Use the sample to review fit, opening action, closure, appearance, assembly sequence and packing method. Ask which details may differ between the sample process and the intended bulk-production process.
